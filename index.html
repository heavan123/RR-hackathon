3 · Methodology in four steps

1Define pressure

Two win-probability models give each delivery a leverage index (expected swing in win chance). Chase pressure = leverage at least 1.5× average (26% of chase balls). First innings pressure = a collapse, wickets at least 1 SD above par for the over (15.6%).

2Measure against par

Every ball is compared with the league outcome in the same innings, phase, pace/spin, wickets bucket and pressure state. Wickets are worth 13.4 runs in the powerplay, 9.7 in the middle, 5.3 at the death.

3Blend and shrink

Empirical-Bayes blend of each player’s pressure record and usual level, weighted by precision. Small samples are pulled to the mean. Consistency (share of matches with positive impact) adds 30% of the score.

4Stress-test

Scores are averaged over four pressure definitions (base, stricter, looser, chase-only). IPL balls weigh 1.0; T20Is 0.8, or 0.4 against Associates. Eligibility: ≥80 pressure balls (bat) or ≥60 (bowl), ≥40 in the IPL, active since 2025.

4 · Why the definition works

Leverage by over, chase (1.0 = average)

What the data says

• In the highest-leverage chase moments the league wicket rate is 7.3% versus about 5% elsewhere, and batters score 1.49 runs a ball versus 1.35–1.38.

• In first-innings collapses scoring falls to 0.98–1.25 runs a ball versus 1.40–1.73 in normal states of the same phase.

• No pressure-specific skill beyond overall quality was detectable. Odd/even-match tests: batters pressure→pressure r = 0.20, baseline→pressure 0.14; bowlers 0.08 and 0.19. Treat the list as a ranked shortlist with overlapping uncertainty.
